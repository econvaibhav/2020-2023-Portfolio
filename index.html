The argument running through the work

Not a move away
from economics.
A wider way to study it.

The subjects change: renewable investment, household health expenditure, education, public-service data. The recurring task does not. Define a question, decide what would count as evidence, and make the analytical choices visible.

The substance matters. Growth is not the same question as distribution. Recorded spending is not the same measure as health. An administrative category is not the social event itself.

The method matters just as much. Models, scraping, microdata and visual design each make some things easier to see and leave other things unresolved. That connection is what this portfolio brings into focus.

01 / Climate & developmentPROJECT

Renewable energy & economic growth

Can renewable investment fit into an account of how economies grow?

A growth-theory question carried through data, specification and research communication.

What changes when an economic model gains a new explanatory variable?

Cross-country data assembly, an augmented Solow specification, alternative proxies and robustness checks. The paper connects Penn World Table, World Bank, IRENA and educational-attainment data; the summary describes a 90-country sample.

Health & livelihoods

How are household amenities related to the financial burden of illness?

Household conditions, treatment settings and expenditure, examined at the unit level.

NSS 71st-round social-consumption health data, recoded household facilities, grouped descriptive tables and regression. The separate report, tables and regression note make it possible to inspect the stages of the analysis rather than only its final narrative.

03 / Visual communicationPROJECT

Making evidence legible

Who can actually read a chart, and what does its design let them see?

Visual accessibility as an analytical concern, with a poster and a Python sketchbook.

How do colour, shape and visual encoding change an explanation?

Colour and marker experiments, statistical plots, maps and chart composition with matplotlib, NumPy, seaborn, Plotly and GeoPandas. The notebook is an exploratory visual lab, including synthetic and randomly generated examples, rather than a single empirical study.

04 / Public-service dataPROJECT

From a public webpage to a dataset

How can an administrative report become material for analysis?

A compact Python pipeline for extracting, cleaning and structuring administrative tables.

What has to happen between an HTML table and usable numeric columns?

Requests retrieves the page; BeautifulSoup selects a table; Python cleans row text; pandas supplies column labels and numerical conversion. A later section also explores an Excel workbook using openpyxl.

Category review, four-state visual comparisons, word-frequency displays and colour-blind-friendly graphics. The closing sections explicitly discuss categorisation, reporting and the interpretation of administrative data.

07 / Incentives & conservationPROJECT

Rebuilding the evidence on water pricing

When is a water-saving practice also an economic incentive?

Eight published tables approached as an exercise in empirical reasoning, not just table production.

What does it take to reconstruct a published table and its meaning?

Table-by-table replication, treatment interactions, fixed effects and village-clustered standard errors as presented in the replication report. The project distinguishes the study design from the interpretation of individual coefficients.

The report uses PLFS 2018–19 data, education categories, employment groups, regression controls and interaction terms. Maps and grouped charts accompany the regression tables, and the methods section describes pandas-based variable preparation.

About this collection

Vaibhav Agarwal

BA Economics, 2020–2023
Azim Premji University

This is a selection of the written, empirical, computational and visual work from my undergraduate portfolio. I have organised it around questions and methods rather than semesters.

The collection shows the foundations of my move towards computer science and social data science: working with imperfect evidence, making data usable, writing analytical logic and communicating what a result can support.

Several projects were collaborative. Their project pages name the contributors recorded in the original work; the documents retain the original author lists, figure credits and references.

Incentives & conservation

Rebuilding the evidence on water pricing

When is a water-saving practice also an economic incentive?

Open the work ↓Replication Regression Study design

01 / Substance

The question behind the work

This replication exercise works through Tables 1–8 of Chakravorty, Dar and Emerick’s 2019 study, Inefficient water pricing and incentives for conservation. The substantive problem is the relationship between irrigation incentives and a water-saving practice.

02 / Methods

What does it take to reconstruct a published table and its meaning?

Table-by-table replication, treatment interactions, fixed effects and village-clustered standard errors as presented in the replication report. The project distinguishes the study design from the interpretation of individual coefficients.

Read for the argument

What the work brings out

The report separates an overall treatment comparison from the interaction with volumetric pricing. That distinction connects the table to the economic question: introducing a practice and changing the incentive to use it are not the same intervention.

Read it in the original · page 6 ↗

The connection

Replication is a different kind of contribution from proposing a new model. It requires tracing what a published result actually rests on, and checking that the output has been matched to the right question.

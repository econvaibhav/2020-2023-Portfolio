# Two development paths, several measures

**What does development look like when GDP is not the only measure?**

## Substance

A visual history of India and China sits alongside two comparative data assignments. The collection moves between life expectancy, fertility, schooling, sectoral employment and output, connecting development questions to more than one indicator.

## Methods

Comparative time-series charts, linear-log regressions and a two-page historical timeline. The first assignment links social indicators to log GDP per capita; the second studies structural change and informal employment.

## What the work brings out

The assignments put social indicators alongside GDP and then separate output shares from employment shares. Read together with the timeline, they make development a question about several changing relationships rather than one national growth curve.

## The connection

The timeline provides context, while the plots make the comparisons explicit. Neither substitutes for the other: historical coincidence is not causal identification, and a single growth statistic is not a full account of development.

01 / Substance

The question behind the work

A poster on colour-blind-friendly visualisation sits beside a Python sketchbook of plotting experiments. Together they treat communication as part of analysis: an argument has to survive the way it is drawn and the way a reader perceives it.

02 / Methods

How do colour, shape and visual encoding change an explanation?

Colour and marker experiments, statistical plots, maps and chart composition with matplotlib, NumPy, seaborn, Plotly and GeoPandas. The notebook is an exploratory visual lab, including synthetic and randomly generated examples, rather than a single empirical study.

Read for the argument

What the work brings out

The poster asks how readers with different colour perception can still follow the same comparison. Its examples turn visual accessibility into a practical design problem, using chart form and additional encodings rather than treating a colour palette as the whole solution.

Read it in the original · page 1 ↗

The connection

The common thread is not a particular plotting library. It is the effort to make differences, comparisons and uncertainty visible without asking every reader to perceive colour in the same way.
